## **WorldStone Justice: Embers and Shadows**

**Scene 1: The Arrival**

---

The air over Seoul tasted of rain and exhaust, a familiar chemical tang that Sung Jin-Woo had long since learned to ignore. But tonight, something else lingered in the atmosphere—a resonance that vibrated against his Monarch senses like a plucked guitar string. It wasn't hostile. It wasn't even directed. It simply _was_, a note of power so pure and foreign that it made the shadows at his feet ripple in unease.

He stood atop the YG Headquarters building, the city sprawled beneath him in a galaxy of artificial light. The disturbance had manifested minutes ago—a silent, geometric tear in reality itself, hovering a hundred meters above the neighboring skyscraper. No sound, no shockwave, just a perfect hexagon of shimmering distortion, like a piece of stained glass hanging in midair.

And from it, a figure fell.

Not fell. _Descended._

She landed on the rooftop edge with the weightless grace of a cat, her silhouette backlit by the geometric rift before it sealed behind her without a sound. Crimson hair, swept back as if by a private wind. Armor that seemed forged from twilight and embers. She didn’t look around, didn’t scan for threats. She simply… observed.

Jin-Woo didn’t hesitate. In the space between heartbeats, he was gone—a teleport fueled by shadow, reappearing directly behind her, one hand already extended, ready to subdue, to question, to _understand_.

His fingers closed on empty air.

She was gone. Not a blur, not a afterimage—just _absent_, as if she had never been there at all.

Jin-Woo stood frozen, his shadow sense stretching out in every direction, through every floor of the building, into the streets below, the sewers, the sky. Nothing. No trace of energy, no displacement of air, no heat signature. It was as though the rooftop had imagined her.

“Odd,” he murmured, his voice flat in the night air. “Very odd.”

The shadows around him coiled tighter, restless. They had felt it too—the absolute, tranquil _authority_ of her presence, and the even more unsettling completeness of her disappearance.

---

A block away, perched on the steel gargoyle of a century-old church steeple, Nimona Harold stifled a giggle behind her gauntleted hand.

“Hmph! That was close,” she whispered to herself, her bright eyes sparkling with mischief. “Wouldn’t want to blow my cover so quickly.”

She watched the rooftop through senses that had nothing to do with sight. She saw the Shadow Monarch’s energy signature—a deep, complex well of void and order, impressive in its control, contained in a way that spoke of discipline and burden. _Not like Reaper_, she thought, a flicker of cold passing through her. _This one’s still sane. Still in charge. Good._

But Kai’s warning echoed in her mind: _“His power is a beacon. If the Dark Knights trace that resonance back to his shadow network, they’ll corrupt it from the inside. He won’t just lose his army. He’ll become their gate.”_

Nimona’s playful expression softened into something more serious. She hadn’t come here to fight. She’d come to assess. To see if Sung Jin-Woo could be reasoned with.

But first… she needed to get a closer look. And for that, she needed to blend in.

---

**The Next Morning – Korea Hunter’s Association, Main Branch**

The line to register as a new Hunter stretched out of the building, down the marble steps, and around the corner. It was a cross-section of desperate humanity—burnt-out office workers, gym rats with more confidence than sense, teenagers dreaming of glory, and grim-faced men and women who’d already seen too much.

Nimona stood near the back, doing her best impression of “ordinary.” She’d changed her appearance—not with magic, but with sheer presence. Her armor was gone, replaced by dark jeans, boots, and a simple red hoodie. Her hair was tied back. She looked like a fit, unusually focused university student. No one gave her a second glance.

She was, however, _profoundly_ bored.

“This is taking forever,” she mumbled, tapping her foot. She’d already counted the bricks on the building across the street twice. She’d tuned in to seventeen different private conversations using a sliver of psychic resonance (mostly complaints about low-level gates and dungeon loot). She’d even subtly heated the air around a particularly annoying man bragging about his nonexistent achievements just to make him sweat.

Finally, after what felt like an eternity of bureaucratic slog, she was ushered into the cavernous testing hall.

The room was clinical and loud, echoing with the hum of mana-measurement orbs, the thud of fists on reinforced targets, and the monotonous drone of evaluators calling out ranks.

“E-Rank. Next.”  
“D-Rank. Proceed to the physical.”  
“F-Rank. Application denied.”

At the front of the room, a tired-looking official sat behind a fortified plexiglass window. On the counter in front of him sat the Standard Mana Resonance Scanner—a bulbous, crystal-based device about the size of a basketball, glowing with a soft blue light.

“Place your hand on the orb,” the official recited without looking up. “Channel your mana. Do not attempt to overload the system. Fraud is punishable by immediate detainment.”

Nimona stared at the machine. Then she stared at her hand. Then she looked back at the machine, a slow, mischievous smile spreading across her face.

_Channel my mana_, she thought. _Yeah, that’d level the city block._

Instead of stepping forward, she took a delicate, almost dainty step _back_. She raised her right hand, pinching her thumb and forefinger together as if holding something tiny and precious.

Between them, a single, marble-sized particle of light coalesced. It was not mana. It was not ki. It was a perfectly contained, self-sustaining mote of **primordial flame essence**, refined to an atomic point. To the naked eye, it looked like a captive red star, radiating a gentle, comforting warmth.

The official finally glanced up. “Miss, you need to touch the—”

“Shhh,” Nimona whispered, her eyes locked on her creation with artistic pride. “Watch.”

She blew gently across her fingertips.

The red particle drifted forward, carried on her breath as lightly as a dandelion seed. It moved with impossible slowness, painting a faint, shimmering trail in the air. The entire room seemed to hush, the other applicants and evaluators unconsciously holding their breath as the beautiful, tiny star floated toward the scanning orb.

It touched the crystal surface.

There was no sound at first.

The blue light of the scanner winked out.

The red particle sank into the crystal, not breaking it, but being _absorbed_.

For one perfect, silent second, the orb turned a breathtaking, translucent crimson, like a giant ruby.

Then the world turned white.

---

It was not an explosion of fire and debris. It was an explosion of **light** and **silent force**. A sphere of pure, annihilating energy erupted from the scanner, consuming it, the counter, the plexiglass, the wall behind it—expanding at the speed of thought.

But Nimona was already moving.

To the frozen people in the room, she didn’t seem to move at all. One moment she was standing there, the next, the world was ending in slow motion. But in the fractured microseconds of the unfolding cataclysm, she was everywhere at once.

She didn’t run. She _flowed_.

- She appeared beside a young woman clutching a child, wrapping them in a cocoon of heatless red light before the shockwave could turn them to ash.
    
- She was at the left wall, shoving three stunned men through the emergency exit with a touch that felt like a warm breeze.
    
- She was in the center of the room, her foot tapping the ground. A ripple of concussive force shot out in a perfect circle, knocking everyone else safely off their feet and away from the epicenter _just_ as the blast wave hit them.
    
- She caught a flying piece of molten shrapnel the size of a car door with one hand, her fingers not even denting the superheated metal before she tossed it neatly up through the hole she’d just made in the ceiling.
    

All of this happened in less than a second. A ballet of absolute, calm, and impossible preservation.

The sphere of energy reached its full diameter—ten meters—and then, as if obeying an unspoken command, **imploded**. It collapsed inward with a deep _WHUMP_ that sucked the air out of everyone’s lungs, and vanished, leaving only a perfectly spherical, glass-smooth crater in the floor and half the testing hall… gone. Vaporized with surgical precision.

Silence.

Dust drifted in the beams of sunlight now pouring through the new hole in the roof. Thirty-seven applicants and staff lay scattered around the edges of the room, coughing, disoriented, but completely unharmed. Not a scratch. Not a burn.

Nimona stood at the edge of the crater, in the exact spot she’d started. She brushed a speck of dust from her hoodie sleeve and looked at the head official, who was staring blankly at the space where his desk used to be.

“Oops,” she said, her voice clear in the ringing quiet. A faint, playful smile touched her lips. “I think your machine might be… defective?”

She turned and walked calmly toward the main entrance, the crowd parting for her like a sea before a ship. No one tried to stop her. No one even spoke.

As she pushed through the double doors back into the sunlight, she chuckled to herself, the sound lost in the rising cacophony of sirens and screams beginning outside.

_Well_, she thought, her eyes scanning the skyline until they landed on the distant, towering silhouette of the Hunter’s Association HQ. _That should get his attention._

And somewhere in that tower, Sung Jin-Woo, the Shadow Monarch, felt a new disturbance on the edge of his senses—not a geometric rift this time, but the after-echo of a power so casually unleashed it had rewritten the local laws of physics, followed by the distinct, unmistakable signature of a predator who had just announced her presence to the whole food chain.

The game had begun.

**Scene 2: Ruin and Recognition**

---

The Hunter’s Association testing hall was now a public spectacle. News vans crowded the street, their satellite dishes rising like metallic flowers. Reporters shouted over one another, cameras flashing, microphones thrust toward the shell-shocked officials trying to maintain a perimeter. The air was thick with the smell of ozone, melted concrete, and human sweat.

Inside the ruined building, the scale of the destruction was even more surreal. The spherical crater in the floor was flawless—a perfect hemisphere of glassy, fused material that gleamed under the emergency lights. Half the room was simply _gone_, not collapsed, but erased, leaving a clean cross-section of the building’s innards exposed like a dollhouse. Wires and pipes dangled into the void, severed with impossible neatness.

Chaos reigned among the lower-ranking officials. No one could explain it. A mana scanner didn’t just _explode_—it was designed to safely dissipate overloads. This was different. This was as if a star had been born and died inside the crystal.

A ripple of silence cut through the din as a new figure arrived. No flashy entrance, no dramatic shadow swirl. One moment the center of the crater was empty, the next, **Sung Jin-Woo** stood there, hands in the pockets of his dark coat, his expression unreadable. Beside him materialized **Woo Jin-Chul**, the stern-faced vice-chairman of the Monitoring Division, looking paler than usual.

“Explain,” Jin-Woo said, his voice low, but it carried through the ruin, silencing the nearest officials.

Jin-Chul cleared his throat, scrolling nervously on a tablet. “Initial witness accounts are… consistent, sir. A new applicant. Female. Crimson hair. She didn’t touch the scanner. She… blew a red particle at it. Then _that_ happened.” He gestured weakly at the crater. “Casualties: zero. Injuries: zero. Structural damage… as you see. She evacuated everyone present before the detonation. We have a name from the destroyed intake form. ‘Nimona.’ No surname. No registration.”

Jin-Woo’s eyes scanned the room, his shadow sense expanding, tasting the residual energy in the air. It was faint, already dissipating, but unmistakable—the same resonant, foreign signature from the geometric rift. Warm, dense, and… alive. It felt less like spent mana and more like a scent left behind by a passing creature.

_She protected them all_, he noted clinically. _Total control. This wasn’t an accident. It was a demonstration._

His gaze stopped near the far edge of the crater, where the ruined wall met what was left of the hallway. There, leaning casually against a jagged spar of broken rebar, was the woman from the rooftop. She was watching the media circus outside through the shattered window, a small, amused smile playing on her lips. She’d changed her clothes, but the crimson hair and the aura of tranquil, contained power were the same.

Jin-Woo moved.

To the observers, he simply vanished from the crater’s center and reappeared directly behind her, his shadow barely whispering against the dusty floor.

“Identify yourself,” he said, his tone flat, allowing no room for evasion.

Nimona didn’t jump. She didn’t even turn her head immediately. Her smile widened a fraction, as if she’d been expecting him.

“I did,” she said, her voice light, almost melodic. “Nimona.” Finally, she glanced over her shoulder, meeting his eyes. Her gaze was unnervingly direct. “You must be the famous Shadow Monarch. Your shadows are… very well-behaved.”

She pushed off the wall and began to walk away, as if the conversation was over.

Jin-Woo’s hand shot out, clamping down on her shoulder. His grip wasn’t brutal, but it was immovable, reinforced by the barest fraction of his strength. “I’m afraid you can’t leave,” he stated, the air around them cooling slightly. “Not until I get some answers.”

That’s when she turned to face him fully.

Her amusement vanished, replaced by something ancient and cold. The change was in the eyes. The crimson irises seemed to deepen, glowing with an inner light. Then, the whites of her eyes **darkened**, not to red, but to an absolute, starless **black**, making the crimson centers blaze like twin embers in a void. The friendly, human pretense melted away, revealing the cosmic entity beneath.

The effect was instantaneous and visceral. The few officials nearby stumbled back, a primal fear seizing them. Jin-Chul froze, his tablet almost slipping from his fingers.

Nimona looked down at the hand on her shoulder, then back up at Jin-Woo. “You should be more careful about what you grab, Monarch,” she said softly. “Some things burn.”

Her free hand came up, not in a strike, but with delicate precision. Her fingers wrapped around his wrist. Her touch was searingly hot, not enough to damage him, but enough to feel like holding a live power cable. With effortless, undeniable force, she peeled his grip from her shoulder.

The motion wasn’t violent, but it was **absolute**. Jin-Woo felt his own strength, enough to crush steel, be casually, gracefully overridden. It wasn’t that she was stronger in raw output—it was the **quality** of the force, refined and focused to a perfect point.

For the first time in a very long time, Sung Jin-Woo felt a flicker of genuine surprise.

Before he could react further, she was gone.

Not a teleport. A **dash** of such impossible speed that the air itself tore. She became a blur of motion, leaving behind a fading trail of **crimson light streaks** that hung in the air like after-images of a comet. They pulsed once with residual heat before dissipating into shimmering nothingness.

She didn’t head for the door or the window. She simply moved _through_ the ruined space in a zig-zag pattern, a path only she could see, and vanished into the crowded Seoul streets beyond, her presence dissolving from Jin-Woo’s shadow sense like smoke in the wind.

Jin-Woo stood alone, staring at the spot where she’d vanished. The faint scent of ozone and something like sun-warmed stone lingered. He slowly looked down at his hand, the one that had held her shoulder. His skin was unmarked, but a phantom warmth remained, a subtle echo of her power.

Woo Jin-Chul hurried over, his face ashen. “Sir! Should I mobilize—?”

“No,” Jin-Woo interrupted, his voice thoughtful. He clenched his hand into a fist, extinguishing the lingering warmth. His dark eyes were fixed on the fading red streaks in the air. “She wasn’t running. She was making a point.”

_‘Some things burn.’_

He had his answers, even if they weren’t the ones he wanted. She was powerful. She was in control. And she was now a variable in his city.

The Shadow Monarch turned, his coat sweeping around him as he addressed Jin-Chul, his tone leaving no room for debate.

“Find her. Quietly. I want to know everything about ‘Nimona.’ Where she eats, where she sleeps, what she looks at. But do not, under any circumstances, attempt to engage her.”

He cast one last look at the perfect, glassy crater—a signature as clear as a calling card.

“This wasn’t an attack,” he said, more to himself than to Jin-Chul. “It was an introduction.”

And introductions, in Jin-Woo’s experience, were often the prelude to a storm.


**Scene 3: The Echo in the Quiet**

---

The walk back to his apartment was a silent one. Sung Jin-Woo moved through the bustling streets of Seoul like a ghost, the vibrant noise of the city—the chatter, the traffic, the distant hum of commerce—muffled beneath the weight of his own thoughts. His shadows, usually a comforting, ever-present extension of his will, felt unusually still. Watchful. As if they, too, were trying to parse the strange signature left behind in that ruined hall.

_Who is she?_

The question echoed in the quiet of his mind, a relentless pulse beneath his calm exterior. He had faced Monarchs, beings of ancient, world-ending power. He had felt the chill of absolute death, the vacuum of pure destruction. Their energies were vast, heavy, _old_.

Hers was… different. It wasn't just power. It was _presence_. A resonant warmth that felt less like stored energy and more like a living, breathing star compressed into human form. It was vibrant, chaotic in potential, yet wielded with a control so absolute it felt serene. The contrast was unnerving. It didn't fit into any category he knew.

He arrived home, the familiar space feeling both like a sanctuary and, for the first time, a potential cage. He sat on the edge of the sofa, not relaxing, simply… existing in the quiet. The phantom warmth on his wrist was gone, but the memory of it remained—the effortless way she had broken his grip. Not with overwhelming force, but with _precision_. It wasn't a contest of strength; it was a lesson in application.

For the first time since he had ascended to the title of Shadow Monarch, Sung Jin-Woo felt a sensation he had almost forgotten.

**Unease.**

It wasn't fear. Fear was for tangible threats, for known quantities. This was the quiet, crawling discomfort of the unknown. A puzzle with pieces that didn't belong to this world. A variable that had entered his equation without his permission, and he didn't yet know its value.

The front door burst open, shattering his reverie. His sister, Jin-Ah, charged into the living room, her eyes wide with a frantic, almost giddy energy. She held her phone out in front of her like a holy relic, her thumb stabbing at the screen.

"Oppa! Oppa, look! You have to see this!"

Before he could respond, she thrust the phone into his line of sight. The screen was a chaotic mosaic of news alerts, social media posts, and blurry, shaky footage. The headlines screamed:

**"MYSTERY 'S-RANK' DESTROYS HUNTER TESTING HALL – ZERO CASUALTIES!"**  
**"WHO IS NIMONA? THE RED-HAIRED ENIGMA SHATTERS THE POWER SCALE!"**  
**"ASSOCIATION IN CHAOS: NEW HUNTER CLASSIFICATION 'NATIONAL LEVEL' DECLARED!"**

The video footage played on a loop—grainy cell phone clips of the perfect spherical crater, the stunned faces of evacuees, and a single, fleeting blur of crimson motion that the cameras couldn't properly capture. Comment sections were on fire. Conspiracy theories bloomed like toxic fungi. Was she a new Monarch? A government weapon? An alien?

Jin-Ah scrolled to an official press release from the Hunter Association, stamped with the Chairman's seal. "They're calling her a _National Level Hunter_," she breathed, her voice a mix of awe and disbelief. "But listen to this part—the news anchors are saying that's just a placeholder! They say it's like… like calling a tsunami a 'big wave.' It's just scratching the surface!"

She tapped another video. This one was a live news conference. At the podium stood Chairman **Go Gun-Hee**, his aged face a mask of solemn gravitas, but his eyes held a glint of something sharper—calculated curiosity. Reporters shouted over each other until his voice, calm and authoritative, cut through.

"The individual known as 'Nimona,'" Chairman Go began, pausing for effect, "is a fascinating case study. Our preliminary analysis of the event's aftermath reveals energy signatures that defy our current mana taxonomy. Her demonstrated capacity for both catastrophic output and… _surgical_ preservation of life is unprecedented."

He leaned slightly into the microphone, his next words delivered with deliberate weight. "For the sake of public assurance, we have assigned a provisional National Level designation. However, let me be clear: our understanding is incomplete. She…"

Another pause. A flicker of something unreadable crossed his face.

**"…may or may not be a competitor to Hunter Sung."**

The newsroom exploded into fresh chaos. Jin-Ah gasped, looking from the screen to her brother's impassive face. "Oppa! He means you! Chairman Go _never_ makes comparisons like that! What does that mean? Is she really that strong?"

But Jin-Woo wasn't listening to his sister anymore. His entire focus was locked on those words, replaying them in his mind.

_May or may not be a competitor to Hunter Sung._

Go Gun-Hee knew. He knew Jin-Woo was the Shadow Monarch. He knew the depth of power that title entailed. The Chairman was a master of implication, of saying nothing while saying everything. For him to publicly float such a comparison, even couched in uncertainty, was a seismic event. It wasn't a statement of fact; it was a **warning flare**. A message from the old fox to Jin-Woo himself: _This one is different. This one is in your league. Be aware._

The unease in Jin-Woo's chest crystallized into something colder, sharper. It wasn't just about an unknown power anymore. It was about recognition. Chairman Go had just placed Nimona on his board, not as a pawn, but as a queen whose moves were yet unknown.

He stood up abruptly, the movement causing Jin-Ah to jump back. "Oppa?"

"Go to your room, Jin-Ah," he said, his voice quieter than usual, devoid of its usual reassuring firmness.

"B-but—"

"Now."

She flinched at the tone—not angry, but _final_. With one last worried look, she scurried away, the forgotten phone left on the couch.

Jin-Woo walked to the large window overlooking the city. The lights of Seoul twinkled, a testament to the order he had helped preserve. Tonight, they looked less like beacons of safety and more like countless points of vulnerability.

In the glass, his reflection stared back—a young man with the eyes of an ancient king. And for a fleeting second, superimposed over his own image, he saw the afterimage of crimson streaks and eyes of fire and void.

Chairman Go had issued a warning. Nimona had issued a challenge.

And Sung Jin-Woo, the Shadow Monarch, who had conquered death itself, felt the unfamiliar but not entirely unwelcome stirrings of a new game beginning. The unease was still there, cold in his veins. But beneath it, something else began to burn—the quiet, focused ember of **anticipation**.

He had questions. And he intended to get his answers. Not as an official. Not as a hunter.

But as the sovereign of the shadows, meeting a creature of living flame on the battlefield she had chosen.


**Scene 4: The Call**

---

The cold anticipation that had settled in Jin-Woo's chest didn't harden into grim resolve. Instead, something more complex unfolded across his features. The stern line of his mouth softened, not into a smile, but into something more thoughtful. His brow furrowed, but it was the furrow of calculation, not anger. A spark of genuine, perilous **curiosity** lit behind his dark eyes, mingling with the **seriousness** of the threat, a flicker of **amusement** at her sheer audacity, and the ever-present, grounding hum of **unease**.

He was a puzzle solver by nature. The System had been a puzzle. The Monarchs had been a puzzle. Now, she was the most intriguing one yet.

His phone, lying silent on the glass coffee table, buzzed.

Not a call. A message. From an unknown number.

He picked it up, his thumb hovering over the screen. There were no identifiers, no carrier information. It was as if the message had simply willed itself into existence on his device.

The preview showed two things:

First, a simple, cheerful emoji: **: )**

Below it, the text:

> **"Sorry for taking your spotlight. You just seemed so bored, so I thought it'd be good to spice a few things up."**

Every instinct, every ounce of his Monarch-honed senses, went razor-sharp. The casual invasion of his privacy was one thing. The tone was another. It wasn't a taunt. It wasn't a threat. It was… conversational. Familiar, even. As if she was commenting on the weather after rearranging the tectonic plates beneath his feet.

His thumb moved, not to type a reply, but to call the number directly. He held the phone to his ear, his expression impassive, listening to the ring.

One. Two.

It picked up.

**"Hello, this is Nimona Harold! How may I help you?"**

Her voice was bright, cheerful, professionally polite—the kind of voice you'd hear answering the phone at a boutique or a nice cafe. It was so utterly, disarmingly normal that it was, in itself, a form of surreal aggression.

Jin-Woo didn't waste a second. His voice was a low, controlled monotone, devoid of the playful curiosity he'd felt moments before. "Where did you get this number?"

On the other end, he could almost hear her pout. "What's wrong with that?" The professional veneer cracked, replaced by a playful, knowing lilt. "Aren't you having fun, _Jinwoo_? 'Cause I sure am."

She laughed then—a clear, genuine sound of delight that felt utterly alien in the context of their silent war of unease and demonstration. She was enjoying this. Truly, simply enjoying it.

The use of his given name, without title or honorific, was a deliberate needle. A reminder that she saw past the titles—Hunter Sung, Shadow Monarch—to the person beneath. And she found him… entertaining.

"I would appreciate it," Jin-Woo said, each word measured and cold, "if you would call me Hunter Sung." He let the pause hang, heavy with unspoken authority. "And one word of advice: Do not stand in my way."

The laughter didn't stop. If anything, it grew warmer, as if he'd just told the funniest joke. When she spoke again, her voice had dropped to a conspiratorial whisper, yet it carried the weight of a promise—or a prophecy.

**"I'll be your shadow."**

The line went dead.

Jin-Woo slowly lowered the phone, staring at the now-blank screen. The words echoed in the silent apartment.

_I'll be your shadow._

It wasn't a declaration of war. It wasn't a submission. It was something far more intimate and disquieting. A shadow isn't an enemy. It isn't an ally. It's a constant, silent companion, shaped by your own light, revealing contours you might not see yourself. It can conceal, or it can betray.

She wasn't threatening to fight him. She was promising to **observe** him. To be his mirror. His contrast. The flame to his darkness.

A slow, very rare, and utterly dangerous smile finally touched Jin-Woo's lips. It didn't reach his eyes, which remained pools of calculated night.

The game was no longer just about identifying a threat or neutralizing a variable. She had redrawn the boundaries. She wasn't a problem to be solved. She was a player who had sat down at his table, picked up the pieces, and declared she would play by her own rules.

_Nimona Harold._

He had a name now. Not just a title or a description. A name belonging to a person who found cosmic-level power displays "fun," who apologized for stealing the spotlight with a smiley face, and who promised to be the shadow of a Shadow Monarch.

The unease was still there, a cold stone in his gut. But it was now surrounded by the kind of focused, electric clarity he hadn't felt since the final gates closed. The city outside felt different. The very air felt charged.

She wanted to spice things up?

Very well.

Sung Jin-Woo put the phone down and walked back to the window. The night city stared back, but now, in every deep alley, in every pooled darkness beneath the streetlights, he imagined a flicker of crimson, a whisper of heat.

_Let's see_, he thought, the shadow at his feet stretching long and eager across the floor. _Let's see just how much heat a shadow can take before it burns._


**Scene 5: An Unwelcome Guest**

---

The morning light streamed through the wall-to-wall windows of the Hunter Association's executive floor, casting long, severe shadows across the polished marble. Sung Jin-Woo moved through the sterile halls with a silence that was less about stealth and more about presence—an atmosphere of quiet authority that preceded him, causing staff to stiffen and find sudden, urgent business elsewhere.

His destination was the Chairman's private office. The public declaration, the casual comparison to _himself_—it was a calculated move by Go Gun-Hee, and Jin-Woo intended to understand the calculation. He needed clarity, and he needed it away from prying eyes and chattering newsfeeds.

He reached the ornate double doors, flanked by two A-Rank hunters whose eyes widened slightly before they snapped to attention. Jin-Woo didn't acknowledge them. He reached for the handle.

From within, he heard a sound that made his hand freeze a centimeter from the brass.

**Laughter.**

A warm, bright, utterly relaxed laugh. Followed by the low, raspy chuckle of Chairman Go himself.

Jin-Woo’s expression, already neutral, went utterly blank. A flicker of something sharp and cold—**annoyance**, pure and undiluted—passed through him before being sealed away. He pushed the door open.

The scene inside was surreal. Chairman Go wasn't behind his imposing mahogany desk. He was sitting in one of the plush visitor chairs by the coffee table, a delicate porcelain cup in hand. And across from him, lounging in the other chair as if she owned it, was **Nimona**.

She was sipping tea, her crimson hair catching the sunlight like spilled wine. She’d traded her casual clothes for something still simple but sharper—dark pants, boots, a fitted jacket. She looked utterly at home, chatting with the most powerful non-monarch human in Korea like they were old friends catching up.

Both looked up as he entered. Go Gun-Hee’s genial smile tightened almost imperceptibly into something more cautious. Nimona’s eyes, that unsettling crimson, lit with open amusement. She didn’t look surprised.

"Ah, Hunter Sung!" Chairman Go said, setting his cup down and rising with a polite, if slightly strained, smile. "An unexpected pleasure. We were just—"

"What is the meaning of this, Mr. Chairman?" Jin-Woo interrupted, his voice a quiet, flat line that cut through the pleasantries. He didn't raise his voice, but the room temperature seemed to drop several degrees. His gaze was fixed on Go, deliberately ignoring the woman in the other chair. "What is _she_ doing here?"

He had come for a private conversation, a meeting between sovereigns to discuss a new, dangerous variable. He had walked into a… tea party. The violation of his expectation was a subtle, effective power play, and it hadn't even been aimed at him. It was just her nature—to be where she wasn't expected, turning gravity and protocol upside down.

Chairman Go cleared his throat, the picture of diplomatic calm, though his eyes darted briefly to Nimona. "Ms. Harold and I have sorted out the… irregularities from yesterday's incident. The paperwork is being expedited. She has expressed a desire to contribute to Korea's security. Given her demonstrated capabilities, she would be a tremendous asset." He paused, his gaze steadying on Jin-Woo. "Especially," he added with careful emphasis, "working alongside a guild of the highest caliber. We were just about to leave for her formal field assessment."

They surged, not as a mob, but as a perfectly coordinated wave—Igris with his soul-blade high, Bellion with gravity-spear poised, Beru with lightning-claws extended, Tusk with crushing fists, Iron with fortress-breaking momentum—all converging on the glowing woman from every vector at once.

Nimona didn’t brace. She **dashed**.

Her speed wasn’t teleportation. It was pure, refined, impossible **kinetics**. She met the first shadow—Igris—head-on. Her empty fist met his massive zweihander. The impact didn’t sound like metal; it sounded like a **stellar core adjusting its fusion**. The blade didn’t break, but Igris was thrown back, his form flickering.

She didn’t stop. She was already spinning, her body bending at angles that defied bone and joint. Bellion’s spear thrust passed through where her torso had been a nanosecond before. Her elbow snapped out, striking his armored chest. The amplified shadow general **vaporized**—not burned, but disassembled into harmless light and dispersing shadow-stuff.

Her dual plasma axes materialized in her hands. She became a whirlwind of white-orange light. A spin, a sweep, a crescent cut. Beru, Iron, Tusk—their forms met the axes and **ceased to be**. Not even ash remained. The very concept of their amplified existence was purified from the local reality.

Three seconds. Five of his strongest generals, amplified to cosmic threat levels, were gone.

She landed, her eyes locking on Jin-Woo across the field. She **dashed** again.

This time, Jin-Woo was ready. He didn’t try to block. As her axe descended in a cleaving arc that would have split the dimension, he **wasn’t there**. He’d shadow-stepped a millimeter to the left, the heat of her blade searing the air where his neck had been.

The axe struck the ground.

The necropolis floor didn’t explode. It **parted**—a clean, kilometers-deep canyon ripped open in the stone, glowing molten at the edges. But before the crack could fully spread, a tide of liquid shadow poured in from all sides, swallowing the wound, sealing it as if it had never been. And from that pooled darkness, a new wave of soldiers erupted—not generals, but hundreds of elite shadows, their claws and blades aimed at her exposed back.

Nimona smiled. Not a grin of madness, but of pure, focused **joy**.

She didn’t turn. She let them come. As the first wave reached her, she moved.

What followed was less a fight and more a **demonstration of martial absolutes**. Her reflexes were beyond insane—they were **prescient**. She leaned back, letting a blade pass millimeters from her throat, then flowed forward, her own axe severing three shadows at the waist. She dropped low, a leg sweep vaporizing a dozen more, then sprang up, spinning in mid-air, axes extended in a halo of annihilation that cleared a circle fifty meters wide.

She was fighting them all, head-on, and winning through sheer, qualitative superiority.

But Jin-Woo was learning. Adapting. He watched, his eyes missing nothing. Five minutes had passed since the gate timer started.

He saw her pattern. She was slowly, inevitably, using the chaos to control the battlefield, herding the shadows, creating lanes of purified space that led… to him.

He clenched the Fangs of Antares tighter. _Come on then._

As if hearing his thought, Nimona stopped clearing mobs. She raised one hand, fingers spread.

**Firestorms** erupted—not wild infernos, but controlled, rotating columns of white flame that tore through the remaining shadow ranks, not to destroy them, but to **displace** them. They were area denial made manifest, carving a perfect, burning corridor straight through his army, leading from her to him.

The next three minutes were a blur even for gods.

Nimona moved. She didn’t appear in front of him. She appeared **everywhere**. A hundred afterimages, a thousand, each from a different angle—above, below, left, right, from diagonal vectors that shouldn’t exist in three-dimensional space. Each afterimage swung an axe. Each swing was real.

Jin-Woo moved in response, a masterpiece of predictive defense. The Fangs of Antares became blurs of void-darkness, parrying, deflecting, meeting every strike. _Clang-clang-CLANG—_the sound was a continuous, deafening chord of colliding absolutes. Void met star-fire. Shadow met light. Every impact shaved fractions of reality away. The space around them began to glitch, showing brief flashes of void or blinding light.

He was holding her off. Barely. But he was holding.

The timer in his mind: **One minute.**

Nimona disengaged from her thousand-angle assault, leaping back. She landed lightly, her axes vanishing. She looked at him, and for the first time, her expression was utterly serene.

“Fun,” she said.

She raised her hand, palm facing the dead sky, then gracefully swept it down, as if brushing dust from a table.

The tsunami of thermal energy swept Jin-Woo and his remaining shadows toward the gate in a silent, unstoppable tide. He didn't fight it head-on — his mastery let him ride the momentum, letting it carry him backward through the shimmering dimensional tear. He shot out into the daylight of the Seoul quarantine zone, tumbling through the air.

Instinct and flawless control took over. Mid-fall, his body twisted, shadows cushioning and redirecting his momentum into a perfect, acrobatic **backflip**. He landed in a low crouch, one knee and one hand braced against the cracked asphalt, the Fangs of Antares still gleaming in his grip. Smooth. Controlled. As if he’d planned the exit all along.

But before the gate could flicker and seal shut behind him—a blur of crimson and white shot through the dimensional maw.

**Nimona.**

She landed lightly just behind him, a few feet to his right, her boots making no sound. Her glow had faded back to a faint ember-hum beneath her skin. She straightened up, brushed imaginary dust from her sleeve, and looked at him with that same serene, playful expression.

The gate collapsed with a final spatial shudder, sealing away the necropolis forever.

Silence fell over the stunned Hunter Association teams. Cha Hae-In stared, her sword hanging limp at her side. Jin-Woo slowly rose to his feet, the Fangs dissolving into trailing shadows. He turned his head, just enough to meet her gaze.

She hadn’t just beaten him to the exit.

She’d let him go first — then followed him out, as casually as stepping through a doorway.

“Ten minutes,” she said, her voice bright in the quiet. “Not bad, Shadow Monarch. You kept me entertained.”

Then she gave him that familiar, mischievous wink.

And before anyone could react—Jin-Woo, the officials, the cameras—she was gone. Not in a dash of flame or a teleport. One moment she was there, the next she simply **wasn’t**, leaving only the faint scent of sun-warmed stone and a circle of thawed, living earth on the cold asphalt where she’d stood.

Jin-Woo didn’t move. He stood staring at the empty space she’d occupied, the warmth of the ground beneath his feet a quiet, lingering proof of her presence.

She hadn’t just controlled the fight.

She’d controlled the **exit**. The narrative. The aftermath.

He had landed like a champion.  
She had landed like a guest who’d just finished a pleasant visit.

And now she was somewhere in _his_ city. Not trapped in a dead dimension. Not running. Just… elsewhere.

Watching.

Waiting.

_I’ll be your shadow_, she’d said.

He hadn’t realized she meant it so literally.

Cha Hae-In finally broke the silence, her voice hushed. “Hunter Sung… what was that?”

Jin-Woo’s eyes stayed fixed on the empty air.

“A reminder,” he said softly, the word carrying in the sudden quiet. “That some things can’t be contained.”

Not even by a Shadow Monarch.
